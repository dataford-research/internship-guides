How to get a UX design internship

The titles to search, the tools to learn, how to build a small portfolio, the interview, and where to find design roles.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

UX design internships ask for something most other internships do not: a portfolio. Recruiters want to see how you think through a problem, and a resume alone rarely gets you an interview. Here is how I would go after a UX or product design internship as a student today.

Know the titles

UX roles go by a lot of names. Search for UX design intern, product design intern, UI/UX intern, interaction design intern, UX research intern, visual design intern, and content design intern. UX research and content design roles often get fewer applicants, so they are worth a look even if you want to end up in design.

Learn the core tools

You do not need to master everything, but you should be comfortable with:

  • Figma for wireframes, mockups, and clickable prototypes
  • A whiteboard or FigJam for sketching flows
  • A simple way to run user tests, like a video call and a short script
  • Basic HTML and CSS, which helps you talk with engineers

Most teams use Figma, so spend most of your time there.

Build a small portfolio

Two or three strong case studies beat ten screenshots. A good case study shows:

  • The problem and who it affects
  • What you learned from talking to real users
  • A few early sketches or ideas you threw away, and why
  • The final design
  • What changed, or what you would measure if it shipped

If you have no client work, redesign a flow from an app you use, like signing up for a campus club or booking a study room. Talk to five students first so the case study is grounded in real feedback. Class projects and hackathon projects count too.

Write a resume that points to your work

Keep it to one page and put your portfolio link at the top. For each project, say what you did and what came out of it:

  • "Interviewed 12 students and redesigned our club sign-up flow, cutting drop-off by about half"
  • "Designed and tested a mobile prototype for a campus food app with 5 rounds of user feedback"

Prepare for the interview

Most UX internship interviews include:

  1. A portfolio walkthrough. Pick your best case study and tell it as a story: problem, research, ideas, decision, result. Keep it to about 15 minutes.
  2. A design exercise. You might get a prompt like "design a better way to split a bill." Ask who the user is before you sketch anything.
  3. Behavioral questions. Have stories ready about feedback you got, a disagreement with a teammate, and a project that did not go as planned.

Practice the walkthrough out loud a few times. Clear storytelling matters as much as the visuals.

Apply beyond the big names

Big tech design internships get thousands of applicants. Agencies, startups, banks, retailers, hospitals, and universities all hire design interns too, and many post those roles only on their own career pages.

To find them, I Got An Internship is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada. Search for design or UX internships and you will see roles that never make it to the big job boards.

Timing

Big tech design internships often open in late summer and early fall. Agencies and smaller companies hire later, often in the spring. If you miss the first wave, keep building your portfolio and keep applying.

A simple plan

  • Pick the titles you will search for, including UX research and content design
  • Get comfortable in Figma
  • Build two or three case studies grounded in real user feedback
  • Put your portfolio link at the top of a one-page resume
  • Practice your portfolio walkthrough out loud
  • Apply to 40 or more roles, including agencies, startups, and non-tech companies
