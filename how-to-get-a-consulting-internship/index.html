Consulting internships

How to get a consulting internship

The titles to search, the types of firms, the recruiting timeline, the case interview, and where to find consulting roles.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Consulting internships are some of the most competitive roles a student can apply for. Firms hire a small class of interns each summer, the interviews follow a format you have to practice, and most of the hiring happens on a tight calendar. The good news is that the process is predictable, so preparation pays off more here than almost anywhere else. Here is how I would go after a consulting internship as a student today.

Know the titles

Search for more than "consulting intern." Try summer associate, business analyst intern, strategy intern, management consulting intern, technology consulting intern, risk advisory intern, operations consulting intern, and economic consulting intern. Many firms also run short diversity or early-identification programs for first and second year students. These often lead straight into an internship interview the following year, so look for them early.

Understand the types of firms

Consulting is a wide field, and the firms hire on different schedules:

  • Strategy firms: the big strategy houses and smaller boutiques. The most selective, with the earliest deadlines.
  • Big 4 and professional services: advisory, risk, and technology consulting arms of the large accounting firms. They hire many more interns.
  • Technology and implementation consulting: firms that help companies roll out software, data, and cloud systems. A good fit if you have a technical background.
  • Economic and litigation consulting: research-heavy work for law firms and regulators. Great for economics and stats majors.
  • Boutique and in-house strategy teams: smaller firms and internal strategy groups at large companies. Less famous, often easier to get an interview.

Apply across more than one type. Most students who land an offer cast a wider net than they planned to.

Learn the timeline early

Strategy firms often open junior summer applications a full year ahead, and many close by early fall. Big 4 advisory and technology consulting roles tend to run a little later, and boutiques hire all year. Make a list of target firms in the spring before you plan to apply, and track every deadline in one place.

Build the skills firms look for

Consulting firms want people who can break down a messy problem, work with numbers, and explain their thinking clearly. You can build those skills now:

  • Get comfortable with Excel: lookups, pivot tables, and simple charts
  • Practice mental math: percentages, growth rates, and quick estimates
  • Learn to structure a problem into a few clear buckets
  • Write short, clear summaries of your work
  • Join a consulting club or take on a pro bono project for a local business or nonprofit

A real project with a real client, even a small one, gives you stories that interviewers love.

Network with intent

Networking matters more in consulting than in most fields. Go to firm info sessions, talk to alumni who work at your target firms, and ask specific questions about their work and their office. Keep notes on who you talked to. Many firms ask in the interview why you want to join them in particular, and those conversations give you a real answer.

Write a resume that fits the role

Keep it to one page. Lead every bullet with a result and a number where you can, like "cut weekly report time by about 40% by building an Excel template." Show leadership, teamwork, and analysis. Firms screen hundreds of resumes for each spot, so make it easy for a reader to see your impact in a few seconds.

Prepare for the case interview

Most consulting interviews have two parts: fit questions and case interviews. In a case, the interviewer gives you a business problem, such as why a company's profits fell, and you work through it out loud. Practice with a partner at least 20 to 30 times before your first real interview. Work on stating a clear structure, doing math out loud without rushing, and ending with a recommendation. For fit questions, prepare a few short stories about leadership, a challenge you worked through, and a time you changed someone's mind.

Apply beyond the famous names

The well-known firms get most of the attention, but there are thousands of consulting internships at boutiques, regional firms, technology consultancies, and in-house strategy teams. Many of them are only posted on the company's own careers page.

To find them, I Got An Internship is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada. Search for consulting, strategy, or business analyst internships and you will see roles that never make it to the big job boards.

A simple plan

  • Pick 15 to 20 target firms across at least three types
  • Put every deadline in one tracker
  • Talk to at least one person at each of your top firms
  • Practice 20 or more cases with a partner
  • Apply to 30 or more roles, including boutiques and in-house teams
