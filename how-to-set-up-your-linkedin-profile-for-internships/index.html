How to set up your LinkedIn profile for internships

A simple photo and headline, a short About section, experience and project bullets, the right skills, and how to use LinkedIn to reach people.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

When you apply for an internship, there is a good chance a recruiter or hiring manager looks you up on LinkedIn before they decide to call you. As a student you probably do not have much work history yet, and that is fine. A clear, complete profile still makes you easier to say yes to. Here is how I would set one up in an afternoon.

Start with a normal photo and a simple headline

You do not need a professional photoshoot. A well-lit photo of your face, against a plain background, where you look like yourself, is enough. Profiles with a photo get far more views than ones without.

Your headline is the line under your name. Most students leave it as "Student at State University." Make it say what you study and what you are looking for:

  • "Computer science junior | Looking for summer 2027 software engineering internships"
  • "Finance and economics sophomore | Interested in investment banking and FP&A internships"
  • "Marketing student | Social media, content, and analytics"

Recruiters search LinkedIn by keywords, so the words in your headline matter.

Write a short About section

Three to five sentences is plenty. Cover what you study, one or two things you have done, what you want to do next, and how to reach you. For example:

"I'm a junior studying information systems at State University. Last year I built a small dashboard for our student government that tracks event attendance, and I worked part time at the campus IT help desk. I'm looking for a data or business analyst internship for summer 2027. The best way to reach me is sara@email.com."

Keep it in plain first person. Skip phrases like "passionate, results-driven self-starter." They do not tell anyone anything.

Fill in experience, even if it is not an internship

Part-time jobs, campus jobs, research assistant work, club leadership, tutoring, and freelance work all count. For each one, add two or three bullet points about what you did and, when you can, a number:

  • "Answered about 30 help desk tickets a week for students and staff"
  • "Grew the club's Instagram from about 200 to 1,000 followers in one semester"
  • "Organized a hackathon with about 120 participants"

These are the same bullets you would put on your resume, so you can copy them over and keep them in sync.

Add projects and coursework

If your experience section is thin, the Projects section helps a lot. Add class projects, personal projects, and hackathon work with a one-line description and a link to GitHub, a portfolio, or a live demo. For technical and analyst roles, a hiring manager would often rather see one real project than a long list of courses.

You can also add 4 to 6 relevant courses under your education entry. Choose the ones that match the internships you want, like statistics, data structures, or financial accounting.

List the skills recruiters search for

Look at 5 to 10 internship postings for the role you want and note the skills that come up again and again. Add the ones you actually have to your Skills section, like Excel, SQL, Python, Figma, or Salesforce. Put the most important ones at the top. Ask a classmate or a past manager to endorse one or two.

Turn on Open to Work for recruiters only

Under "Open to," you can tell LinkedIn you are looking for internships, with the job titles and locations you want. You can choose to show this only to recruiters, so it does not add the green banner to your photo. It helps your profile show up when recruiters search for interns.

Use LinkedIn to reach people, not only to be found

A profile is only half of it. After you apply for an internship, look up one or two people at that company, like a recent intern or someone on the team, and send a short connection note:

"Hi Jordan, I'm a junior at State University and just applied for the analytics internship on your team. I'd love to hear what the internship was like if you have 10 minutes sometime. Thanks!"

Not everyone will reply, and that is normal. Even a few replies a month can turn into referrals.

Do not rely on LinkedIn alone for finding openings

LinkedIn job listings only show the roles that companies choose to post there, and popular postings get hundreds of applicants within a day. Many internships are posted first, or only, on the company's own careers page. I built I Got An Internship for that reason. It is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada, about 5,000 roles at a time, with filters for role, term, city, and visa sponsorship. I use LinkedIn to find people and the career pages to find the openings.

Quick checklist

  • Clear photo and a headline with your major and the internships you want
  • A three to five sentence About section in plain first person
  • Experience bullets with numbers, including part-time and campus jobs
  • Projects with links, plus 4 to 6 relevant courses
  • Skills that match real internship postings
  • Open to Work turned on for recruiters only
  • A short note to one or two people after each application you care about
