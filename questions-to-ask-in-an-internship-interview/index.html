Questions to ask at the end of an internship interview

Questions about the work, the team, growth, the interviewer, and next steps, plus the ones to skip.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Almost every internship interview ends the same way: "Do you have any questions for us?" A lot of students say no, or ask something they could have found on the company website. That last few minutes is a real chance to learn whether you want the job and to leave the interviewer with a good final impression. Here are the questions I would keep ready, grouped by what they help you learn.

Questions about the work

  • What would a typical week look like for an intern on this team?
  • What project would I most likely work on, and who would use it?
  • What does a successful internship look like by the end of the summer?
  • What tools or skills do interns usually pick up in the first few weeks?

These show you are already picturing yourself in the role, and the answers tell you if the work is real or mostly busywork.

Questions about the team and manager

  • How big is the team, and who would I work with day to day?
  • How often do interns meet with their manager?
  • How do people on the team usually give feedback?
  • Is there a mentor or buddy for interns besides the manager?

A good manager and regular feedback matter more than the company name on your resume. If the interviewer struggles to answer these, that is useful to know.

Questions about growth and return offers

  • What have past interns on this team gone on to do?
  • Does the company usually make return offers to interns?
  • Are there chances to present your work to people outside the team?

Asking about return offers is fine. It shows you are thinking long term, and it helps you compare offers later.

Questions about the interviewer

  • What do you like most about working here?
  • What has surprised you since you joined?
  • What is one thing you wish you knew in your first month?

People like talking about their own experience, and these often turn the end of the interview into a real conversation.

Questions about next steps

  • What are the next steps in the process?
  • When should I expect to hear back?
  • Is there anything else I can send you that would help?

Always ask at least one of these. Knowing the timeline means you know when to follow up and you are not left guessing.

Questions to skip

  • Anything you can find in two minutes on the company website
  • Pay, hours, or time off in a first-round interview, unless the interviewer brings it up
  • "Did I get the job?"
  • Questions that sound like a test, such as "What is the company's biggest weakness?"

Pay and logistics are important, but they are usually easier to discuss with the recruiter once you have an offer or reach a later round.

How many to ask

Plan five or six, and expect to ask two or three. Some will get answered during the interview, so keep a short list on paper or in a note and cross them off as you go. If the interviewer is short on time, pick the one you care about most plus a next-steps question.

Keep applying while you wait

Even after a great interview, keep your search going until you have an offer in writing. I built I Got An Internship to make that part easier. You can filter by role, term, city, and visa sponsorship, and listings come down when the employer removes the posting.

Quick checklist

  • Prepare five or six questions before every interview
  • Ask about the work, the team, and what success looks like
  • Ask the interviewer about their own experience
  • Always ask about next steps and timing
  • Skip anything you can find on the website
  • Save pay and logistics for the recruiter or a later round
  • Send a short thank-you note within a day
