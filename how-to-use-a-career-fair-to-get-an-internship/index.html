Career fairs

How to use a career fair to get an internship

How to prepare a week early, what to say at the table, questions worth asking, and how to follow up after.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Career fairs can feel rushed and awkward. Hundreds of students, long lines, and a few minutes with each recruiter. But a fair is one of the few times employers come to you, and a good two-minute conversation can turn into an interview faster than an online application. Here is how I would prepare, what to say at the table, and what to do in the 48 hours after.

Get the employer list a week early

Most schools post the list of attending employers on Handshake or the career center site about a week before the fair. Go through it and sort employers into three groups:

  • 5 to 8 top choices you will definitely visit
  • 5 to 10 backups you will visit if lines are short
  • Everyone else you can skip

For each top choice, check their careers page and note which internships they are hiring for, the locations, and whether they sponsor visas if that matters to you. Many employers at a fair are recruiting for roles that are already posted, so you can often apply before you even walk in.

Prepare a 30-second introduction

Recruiters talk to a lot of students. Make it easy for them to remember you. A simple structure:

  1. Your name, year, and major
  2. One thing you have done that is relevant, like a project, job, or club role
  3. What you are looking for and why their company

For example: "Hi, I'm Sara, a sophomore studying information systems. Last semester I built a dashboard for our student government that tracks event attendance. I'm looking for a data or business analyst internship for next summer, and I saw your team is hiring for that in Chicago."

Practice it out loud a few times so it sounds natural, not memorized.

Bring the right things

  • 10 to 15 printed copies of your resume, one page, on plain paper
  • A folder so they do not get bent
  • A short list of your top employers and one question for each
  • A phone with your LinkedIn profile ready to share
  • Water and something to eat if the fair runs for hours

Dress one step more formal than the company's normal style. Business casual works for most fairs unless your school says otherwise.

Ask questions you cannot answer with Google

Skip "What does your company do?" Ask things that show you did your homework and help you decide whether to apply:

  • "What do interns on your team usually work on?"
  • "How many interns did you bring on last summer, and how many got return offers?"
  • "Is there anything that makes an application stand out for this role?"
  • "What is the best way to follow up after today?"

The last question matters most. Some recruiters want you to apply online and mention the fair. Others will hand you a card and ask you to email them.

Write things down right after each table

Step away from the line and take 30 seconds to note the recruiter's name, what you talked about, and what they told you to do next. After 10 conversations, it all blurs together. These notes make your follow-up email specific instead of generic.

Follow up within 24 to 48 hours

Send a short email to anyone who gave you their contact info. Keep it to a few lines:

"Hi Jordan, thanks for talking with me at the State University career fair on Tuesday. I enjoyed hearing about the analytics internship and the project your interns did on customer churn. I applied for the role today and attached my resume here. I'd be glad to answer any questions. Best, Sara"

Then actually apply through the official posting if you have not already. A great conversation does not help much if your application is not in the system.

Do not stop at the fair

A career fair only shows you the employers who paid to attend. Plenty of companies hire interns without ever coming to your campus. After the fair, keep searching more widely. I built I Got An Internship for this part. It is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada, about 5,000 roles at a time, with filters for role, term, city, and visa sponsorship.

Quick checklist

  • Review the employer list a week early and pick your top 5 to 8
  • Apply online to posted roles before the fair when you can
  • Practice a 30-second introduction
  • Bring 10 to 15 resumes and one question per top employer
  • Take notes after each conversation
  • Send follow-up emails within 48 hours and apply through the official posting
  • Keep searching beyond the employers who came to campus
