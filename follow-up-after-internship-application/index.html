How to follow up after an internship application

When to send it, who to send it to, and two short templates you can copy after an online application or an interview.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

You hit submit, and then nothing happens for two weeks. That silence is normal. Recruiters running internship programs are often reading hundreds of applications for a handful of seats, and a lot of them review in batches. A short, polite follow-up can still help, as long as you send it to the right person at the right time.

Here is the approach I recommend to students, with templates you can copy.

When to follow up

  • After applying online: wait about one to two weeks. If the posting lists a deadline, wait until a few days after it closes.
  • After a recruiter call or first interview: send a thank-you note within a day, then follow up about a week after the date they said you would hear back.
  • After a final round: if they gave you a timeline, wait until it passes, then send one check-in.

One follow-up per stage is enough. If you still hear nothing after that, mark it as ghosted in your tracker and spend the energy on new applications.

Who to email

Follow up with a real person, not a no-reply address. In order of preference:

  1. The recruiter or coordinator who contacted you.
  2. A campus recruiter for the company, often listed on your school's career platform or at career fairs.
  3. Someone you met at an info session or coffee chat.
  4. A team member you have an honest connection to, like a fellow alum.

Do not cold-message five employees at once asking about your status. It reads as pressure, and they usually cannot see the applicant system anyway.

What to write

Keep it under 100 words. Say who you are, which role, when you applied, one line on why you fit, and a simple question.

After an online application:

> Subject: Data Analyst Intern, Summer 2027 application

>

> Hi Jordan,

>

> I applied for the Data Analyst Intern role on November 3 and wanted to share my continued interest. I am a junior studying statistics, and last summer I built a weekly sales dashboard in SQL and Tableau for a local retailer. I would love to be considered. Is there anything else I can send that would help?

>

> Thanks,

> Sam

After an interview:

> Subject: Thank you, Data Analyst Intern interview

>

> Hi Jordan,

>

> Thanks for taking the time to talk today. I enjoyed hearing how the team uses experiments to plan new features, and it made me more excited about the role. Please let me know if I can share anything else.

>

> Best,

> Sam

Swap in your own details. A specific line about the conversation is what separates a real note from a template.

What not to do

  • Do not ask "did you get my application?" The system confirmed it.
  • Do not attach your resume again unless they asked.
  • Do not follow up the day after you apply.
  • Do not guilt or pressure ("I have not heard anything in weeks").

Keep it in your tracker

Add a follow-up date column next to the date you applied, and a short note on who you contacted. When you do your weekly review, sort by follow-up date and send the ones that are due. That way no application slips, and you never send two follow-ups to the same person by accident.

Keep applying while you wait

The best thing you can do while waiting is keep your pipeline full. One reply, or none, should not decide your whole season.
