Return offers

How to turn your internship into a full-time offer

Ask how return offers work, send weekly updates, meet people outside your team, finish strong, and ask directly before you leave.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

For a lot of students, the best path to a first full-time job is a return offer from their internship. The company already knows you, you already know the team, and both sides skip a long hiring process. Getting that offer is mostly about a handful of habits you can start on day one. Here is what I would focus on.

Ask how return offers work in your first week

Every company handles this differently. Some make offers to most interns who meet the bar, some only have a few full-time spots, and some do not hire interns full time at all. Ask your manager or recruiter early:

  • Does the team usually make return offers to interns?
  • When are those decisions made, and who makes them?
  • What does a strong intern look like on this team?

The answers tell you what to aim for, and asking shows you are thinking about the long term.

Get clear on your project

Most interns get one main project. Within your first few days, write down what done looks like, who the project is for, and when the check-ins are. Then repeat it back to your manager in a short message: "Here is what I understand the goal to be and how I plan to get there. Did I miss anything?" That one message saves a lot of wasted weeks.

Send a short weekly update

Every Friday, send your manager three or four lines:

  • What I finished this week
  • What I am working on next week
  • Where I am stuck or need a decision

Managers are busy and often forget what interns are doing. A weekly update keeps your work visible, and when it is time to write your review, your manager has a record of everything you did.

Ask questions the right way

Asking questions is expected. What helps is showing that you tried first. Something like: "I am trying to pull last month's numbers. I checked the dashboard and the team docs but could not find the source table. Do you know who owns it?" That shows effort and gets you an answer faster.

Meet people outside your team

Your manager matters most, but other people often have a say in return offers. Set up short 15 to 20 minute chats with people on nearby teams, other interns, and anyone who did your internship in a past year. Ask what they work on and what they wish they knew as an intern. These conversations also help if your team does not have headcount but another team does.

Finish strong

The last few weeks are what people remember. Plan to have your project done with time to spare, and prepare a short final presentation:

  • The problem you worked on
  • What you did
  • The result, with a number if you have one
  • What you would do next

Practice it a couple of times. A clear ten minute presentation can do a lot for how your whole summer is remembered.

Ask directly before you leave

In your last week, ask your manager for feedback and ask about next steps: "I have really enjoyed working here and I would love to come back full time. Is there anything I should do to be considered?" Then send a thank-you note to your manager and the people who helped you, and connect with them on LinkedIn.

Keep your options open

Even if things go well, keep applying in the fall. Return offer timelines can be slow, team budgets change, and having other interviews gives you more confidence either way. Quick checklist

  • Ask how return offers work in week one
  • Write down what done looks like for your project
  • Send a short update every Friday
  • Show what you tried before asking for help
  • Meet people outside your team
  • Finish early and practice your final presentation
  • Ask for feedback and next steps before you leave
  • Keep applying until you have an offer in writing

Always get the details of a full-time offer, including start date, pay, and location, in writing before you accept it.
