How to get an internship with a low GPA

Where GPA matters, when to leave it off your resume, what to show instead, and how to answer if it comes up.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

A lot of students assume a GPA under 3.0 rules them out of internships. It does make some applications harder, mostly at large companies that use a GPA cutoff to filter thousands of resumes. But most employers care more about whether you can do the work, and there are plenty of ways to show that. Here is how I would approach the search if my GPA was not where I wanted it.

Know where GPA actually matters

GPA matters most in a few places:

  • Large banks, consulting firms, and some big tech programs that get tens of thousands of applications
  • Postings that list a hard minimum, like "3.0 or higher required"
  • Some government and research programs with formal eligibility rules

Outside of those, many employers either do not ask or treat GPA as one small part of the picture. Small and mid-size companies, startups, nonprofits, and local employers usually care more about skills, projects, and how you come across in an interview. That is where I would spend most of my applications.

Leave it off your resume if it is under 3.0

You do not have to list your GPA. A common rule of thumb is to include it only if it is 3.0 or higher. If your major GPA is stronger than your overall GPA, you can list that instead and label it clearly, for example "Major GPA: 3.4". Never round up or guess. If an application form asks for GPA directly, answer honestly.

Fill the space with proof you can do the work

When GPA is missing, the rest of your resume has to carry more weight. The strongest things to add:

  • One or two real projects with a result, like a dashboard, an app, a research write-up, or a marketing campaign for a club
  • Part-time jobs, even if they are not in your field. Showing up, handling customers, and managing time all count
  • Leadership in a club or team, with something specific you did
  • Relevant coursework where you did well
  • Certifications or short courses tied to the role

Write each bullet around what you did and what changed because of it. "Built a sales tracker in Google Sheets used by 6 people on the team" says more than any GPA.

Apply where your story fits

Instead of sending the same resume to 100 big-name programs, split your list:

  • A small number of reach applications at well-known companies
  • Most of your applications at smaller companies, startups, and local employers
  • A few at places you have a personal connection to, like a former employer, a professor's lab, or a company an alum works at

Smaller companies often hire interns with less process, and a referral or a direct email to the hiring manager can get you past the resume screen entirely.

Use networking to skip the filter

A GPA cutoff usually lives in an online application system. A referral or a conversation with someone on the team can get your resume read by a person instead. Reach out to alumni from your school, people you met at career fairs, and former coworkers. Keep the ask small: a 15-minute call to learn about their team. If it goes well, asking whether they would be open to referring you is a normal next step.

Be ready if it comes up in an interview

If an interviewer asks about your grades, give a short, honest answer and move forward. For example: "My first two semesters were rough while I was working 25 hours a week. Since then my grades have gone up, and I have focused on projects like the one I mentioned." Do not over-explain or blame anyone. Show what you learned and point back to what you can do now.

Work on the trend

An upward trend helps. If you still have semesters left, a stronger term or two gives you something better to say, and it can bring your GPA over a cutoff for next season. Even a few strong grades in courses related to the internship can help in conversations.

Keep your search wide

With a low GPA you will probably need more applications than a classmate with a 3.8, so make the search itself as easy as possible. I built I Got An Internship for this. It is a free internship search platform that pulls internships and co-ops straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada, about 5,000 roles at a time. You can filter by role, term, city, and visa sponsorship, which makes it easier to find smaller employers you might not have heard of.

Quick checklist

  • Focus most applications on small and mid-size companies, startups, and nonprofits
  • Leave GPA off your resume if it is under 3.0, or list your major GPA if it is higher
  • Add projects, jobs, and leadership with specific results
  • Use alumni and referrals to get past online filters
  • Prepare a short, honest answer if grades come up
  • Aim for an upward trend in the semesters you have left
  • Apply wide and track every application
