Finance internships

How to get a finance internship

The main types of finance roles, the skills that matter, how the interview works, and where to find internships beyond Wall Street.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Finance internships have a reputation for being hard to get, and the big banking programs really are competitive. But "finance" covers far more than investment banking. Banks, insurance companies, accounting firms, credit unions, startups, hospitals, and almost every mid-size company have finance teams that hire interns. Here is how I would approach it as a student today.

Know the main types of finance internships

Before you apply, it helps to know what the roles actually are:

  • Investment banking: building models and pitch materials for deals. Long hours, very early recruiting.
  • Sales and trading or markets: working with traders and clients on a trading floor.
  • Corporate finance: budgeting, forecasting, and reporting inside a regular company. Often called FP&A.
  • Commercial banking and credit: analyzing whether businesses can repay loans.
  • Wealth management and financial planning: supporting advisors who manage money for clients.
  • Accounting and audit: often grouped with finance, and one of the biggest sources of internships.

Corporate finance and commercial banking get far fewer applications than investment banking, and they lead to great careers too.

Learn the core skills

You do not need a finance major, but you do need a few basics:

  • Excel: lookups, pivot tables, and building a simple model without getting lost.
  • The three financial statements: how the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement connect.
  • Basic valuation: what a discounted cash flow is and why companies get compared on multiples.

Free courses and YouTube cover all of this. A weekend spent building a small model of a public company you like teaches more than reading about it.

Show interest on your resume

Finance recruiters look for signs that you actually care about the field. A few ways to show it:

  • Join your school's investment or finance club and take a role, even a small one.
  • Write a one-page stock pitch on a company and keep it ready to talk about.
  • Mention any job where you handled money, budgets, or numbers, like a treasurer role or retail cash handling.

Keep the resume to one page and put numbers on every line you can. "Managed a $12,000 club budget across 20 events" says a lot more than "detail oriented."

Prepare for the interview

Finance interviews usually mix behavioral and technical questions:

  1. "Why finance?" and "Why this firm?" Have a short, honest answer for each.
  2. "Walk me through the three financial statements." Practice saying this out loud.
  3. "Pitch me a stock." Use the one-page pitch you already wrote.
  4. "Tell me about a time you worked under pressure." Have two short stories ready.

For corporate finance roles, expect more questions about Excel, budgeting, and how a business makes money, and fewer about deals.

Timing

Big banks recruit very early. Many open summer internship applications more than a year ahead, so sophomores often apply for the summer after junior year. Corporate finance, insurance, credit unions, and smaller firms hire much later, often from January to April. If you missed the early bank deadlines, there are still plenty of roles in the spring.

Look past Wall Street

Search for different titles: finance intern, FP&A intern, financial analyst intern, accounting intern, credit analyst intern, and treasury intern. Many of these sit at companies you would not think of as finance employers.

To find them, I Got An Internship is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada. Search for finance internships in your city and you will see roles that never show up on the big job boards.

A simple plan

  • Pick one or two types of finance roles to focus on
  • Learn Excel, the three statements, and basic valuation
  • Write one stock pitch and join a finance club
  • Apply to big programs early and keep applying to smaller firms in the spring
  • Send 50 or more applications across banks, companies, and local firms
