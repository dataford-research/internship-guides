Accounting internships

How to get an accounting internship

The titles to search, public accounting vs industry, the recruiting timeline, the skills to build, and where to find accounting roles.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Accounting internships are one of the most structured internship paths out there. Firms hire interns on a predictable calendar, a lot of those interns get full-time offers, and the skills you need are clear. The hard part is that the timeline starts earlier than most students expect. Here is how I would go after an accounting internship as a student today.

Know the titles

Search for more than "accounting intern." Try audit intern, tax intern, assurance intern, advisory intern, staff accountant intern, accounts payable intern, financial reporting intern, corporate accounting intern, and forensic accounting intern. Many firms also run "leadership programs" or "discovery programs" for first and second year students, which are short programs that often lead to an internship offer the following year.

Understand the two main paths

Most accounting internships fall into one of two buckets:

  • Public accounting: audit, tax, and advisory work at accounting firms, from the Big 4 down to regional and local firms. These programs are the most structured and hire the most interns.
  • Industry: accounting and finance teams inside companies, hospitals, universities, nonprofits, and government agencies. Hours are often more predictable and the work is closer to how a business runs day to day.

Neither path is better. Public accounting is the most common route toward a CPA license, while industry roles can be easier to land if you start late.

Learn the timeline early

This is where most students lose out. Large firms often recruit for summer and winter busy season internships a full year ahead, and many fill a big share of spots through campus recruiting events and early programs. If you want a public accounting internship for the summer after junior year, start going to firm events and info sessions in your sophomore year. Smaller firms and industry roles hire later, often in the spring.

Build the skills firms look for

You do not need to be an expert, but you should be comfortable with:

  • Intro financial and managerial accounting, with good grades in both
  • Excel: formulas, lookups, pivot tables, and keeping a clean worksheet
  • Reading a balance sheet, income statement, and cash flow statement
  • Basic data tools if you can, like Power BI, Tableau, or a little SQL

Firms also care about your plan for the CPA. Know roughly how many credit hours your state requires and how you plan to get there.

Get involved on campus

Accounting recruiting is very relationship driven. Join your school's accounting society or Beta Alpha Psi chapter, go to Meet the Firms nights, and talk to the same recruiters more than once. Write down who you met and follow up within a day. Recruiters remember students who show up and ask good questions.

Write a resume that fits the role

Keep it to one page and put your GPA on it if it is 3.0 or higher, since many firms screen on it. Show numbers where you can:

  • "Reconciled 200+ monthly transactions for a campus club budget of $15k"
  • "Prepared 30+ basic tax returns as a VITA volunteer"
  • "Built an Excel model that cut monthly reporting time by 2 hours"

Volunteer tax prep through VITA is one of the best things you can add if you have no accounting experience yet.

Prepare for the interview

Most accounting internship interviews are behavioral. Expect questions like "why accounting," "why audit or tax," "why our firm," and "tell me about a time you worked on a team." Have three or four stories ready and know something specific about each firm you talk to. Some interviews add basic technical questions, like how the three financial statements connect or what a journal entry for a common transaction looks like.

Apply beyond the Big 4

The Big 4 get a huge number of applicants. Regional and local firms, mid-size firms, banks, insurance companies, hospitals, government agencies, and companies of every size all hire accounting interns, and many post those roles only on their own career pages.

To find them, I Got An Internship is a free internship search tool that pulls openings straight from about 2,200 employer career pages across the US and Canada. Search for accounting, audit, or tax internships and you will see roles that never make it to the big job boards.

A simple plan

  • Pick the titles you will search for, including audit, tax, and industry roles
  • Start going to firm events and early programs in your sophomore year
  • Get comfortable with Excel and the three financial statements
  • Join your accounting society and follow up with every recruiter you meet
  • Add VITA or a club treasurer role if you need experience
  • Apply to 30 or more roles, including regional firms and companies outside public accounting
