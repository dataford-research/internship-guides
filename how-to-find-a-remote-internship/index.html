How to find a remote internship

What remote means on a posting, where to search, how to show you can work on your own, and how to spot scams.

By Amney Mounir. Published November 16, 2026.

Remote internships are a great option if you can't move for the summer, need to stay close to family, or want to work while taking classes. They are also harder to find than they used to be, and the good ones get a lot of applicants. Here is how I would search for one today.

Know what "remote" means on a posting

Not every posting that says remote is fully remote. Before you apply, check which of these it is:

  • Fully remote: you can work from anywhere in the country, sometimes with a time zone requirement
  • Remote in one state or region: you have to live in a specific state, often for tax or legal reasons
  • Hybrid: you go into an office a few days a week
  • Remote for part of the summer: you start remote and move on site later

Read the location line and the first few paragraphs of the description. If it is unclear, it is fine to ask the recruiter.

Search the right way

Typing "remote internship" into a big job board gives you a lot of noise. A few things that work better:

  • Filter by location set to remote, then by role, so you only see the jobs you would actually take
  • Search employer career pages directly. Many companies post remote roles on their own site long before they show up anywhere else
  • Look at companies that are already remote first. If the whole team works remotely, their interns usually do too
  • Check your school's career site, since some employers only post there
  • Set alerts so you see new postings in the first few days, when there are fewer applicants

Show you can work on your own

The biggest worry a manager has about a remote intern is whether you will get stuck and stay quiet. Your application should answer that before they ask. Mention:

  • Projects you finished on your own, like a class project, a side project, or a club role
  • Times you worked with people in other places or on a different schedule
  • Tools you already use, like Slack, Google Docs, GitHub, Notion, or Zoom

One line like "I built and shipped a small web app on my own over winter break" does more than saying you are self-motivated.

Do well in a video interview

Most remote internships hire over video. Test your camera, mic, and internet the day before. Sit somewhere quiet with good light in front of you, not behind you. Keep a short list of your stories next to the screen, and look at the camera when you answer, not at your own face.

Watch out for scams

Remote listings get more scams than in-person ones. Be careful if a posting:

  • Asks you to pay for training, equipment, or a background check
  • Offers a job without any interview
  • Only talks to you over text or a messaging app
  • Has an email address that does not match the company's website

When in doubt, find the role on the company's own careers page. If it is not there, skip it.

Make the most of it once you start

Remote interns can get forgotten. Set a weekly 1:1 with your manager, send a short update at the end of each week with what you did and what is next, and ask to meet two or three people on other teams. Those habits make it a lot easier to get a strong reference or a return offer.

Quick checklist

  • Confirm what kind of remote the posting means
  • Search by role and remote location, and check employer career pages directly
  • Apply in the first week a role is posted
  • Show examples of work you finished on your own
  • Test your setup before video interviews
  • Never pay to get an internship

Always confirm the pay, hours, location rules, and equipment details in writing before you accept an offer.
